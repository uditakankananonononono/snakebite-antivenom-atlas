ORPHAN|None|None|This Appendix lists venomous snake species considered to represent the
ORPHAN|None|None|greatest threat to public health in various countries, territories and other areas or
ORPHAN|None|None|regions around the world. Only species which fall into one of the two categories
ORPHAN|None|None|listed below are shown, and category listings are in alphabetical order according
ORPHAN|None|None|to taxonomic family, genus and species. The intention in categorizing these
ORPHAN|None|None|medically important snakes into two groups is to provide users of these WHO
ORPHAN|None|None|Guidelines with a prioritized listing. Snakes in both Category 1 and Category 2
ORPHAN|None|None|are species for which antivenom production is important; however species
ORPHAN|None|None|listed in Category 1 within a country, territory or area should be considered as
ORPHAN|None|None|being of highest priority for antivenom production on the basis that available
ORPHAN|None|None|knowledge implicates them as being responsible for the greater burden in that
ORPHAN|None|None|particular setting.
ORPHAN|None|None|Definitions of the categories used in this listing are:
ORPHAN|None|None|■■ CATEGORY 1 (CAT 1): Highest medical importance
ORPHAN|None|None|Definition: highly venomous snakes which are common or widespread
ORPHAN|None|None|and cause numerous snake-bites, resulting in high levels of morbidity,
ORPHAN|None|None|disability or mortality.
ORPHAN|None|None|■■ CATEGORY 2 (CAT 2): Secondary medical importance
ORPHAN|None|None|Definition: highly venomous snakes capable of causing morbidity,
ORPHAN|None|None|disability or death, but: (a) for which exact epidemiological or clinical
ORPHAN|None|None|data may be lacking; and/or (b) are less frequently implicated (owing
ORPHAN|None|None|to their activity cycles, behaviour, habitat preferences or occurrence in
ORPHAN|None|None|areas remote from large human populations).
ORPHAN|None|None|There are numerous other venomous species that rank as lesser threats
ORPHAN|None|None|in countries territories and other areas listed here, and interested readers should
ORPHAN|None|None|refer to the herpetological references provided in these Guidelines. It should be
ORPHAN|None|None|noted that over time, as more information becomes available, new species will
ORPHAN|None|None|doubtlessly be added to these lists, and some species, currently defined within
ORPHAN|None|None|Category 1 or Category 2 will be re-ranked.
ORPHAN|None|None|It should also be noted that the organization of countries, territories and
ORPHAN|None|None|other areas in this Appendix does not follow the WHO regional organization, but
ORPHAN|None|None|instead is arranged biogeographically in alphabetical order of country, territory
ORPHAN|None|None|or geographical area. This approach was necessary to reflect the geographical
ORPHAN|None|None|distribution of major groups of venomous snakes throughout the world. For
ORPHAN|None|None|example, the venomous snakes of the eastern Indonesian Province of Papua have
ORPHAN|None|None|biogeographical origins in Australo-Papua, and are evolutionarily distinct from
ORPHAN|None|None|the venomous snakes of Asian origin that occur west of Wallace’s Line, which runs
ORPHAN|None|None|south of the Philippines, between Borneo and Sulawesi, and between Bali and
ORPHAN|None|None|Lombok, and which separates the zoogeographical regions of Asia and Australia.
ORPHAN|None|None|For this reason, the medically important snakes of Indonesian Papua are listed in
ORPHAN|None|None|the Australo-Papuan region, rather than the South-East Asian region.
ORPHAN|None|None|Users of this Appendix should also recognize that the relative risk of
ORPHAN|None|None|injury from a particular species may vary from one country, territory or area to
ORPHAN|None|None|another. For this reason, some species that have been listed under Category 1
ORPHAN|None|None|in one country, territory or area may have been listed under Category 2 in
ORPHAN|None|None|another country, territory or area to reflect the different risk posed by that species
ORPHAN|None|None|in different locations. Assignment to Category 1 or Category 2 was based in some
ORPHAN|None|None|cases on the relative importance of a species as a cause of snake-bite. In Europe,
ORPHAN|None|None|for example, the overall incidence of snake-bite is trivial compared to that in
ORPHAN|None|None|West Africa or India, but where a European species (such as Vipera berus) is a
ORPHAN|None|None|major (or sole) cause of envenoming where it occurs, this warrants ranking it as
ORPHAN|None|None|a medically important species in that setting.
